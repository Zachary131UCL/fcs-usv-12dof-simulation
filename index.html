01 / GEOMETRY AND REFERENCE FRAMES

Define Geometry, Frames, and 12 Degrees of Freedom Completed

USV occupies the first six degrees of freedom and FCS the last six. Each body uses surge, sway, heave, roll, pitch, and yaw, in that order. Translation is in meters; small rotations are in radians. Global +x points forward, +y to port, and +z upward. The mean free surface is z = 0, and rotations are referenced to each body's center of gravity.

q = [qUSV; qFCS],   qi = [x, y, z, φ, θ, ψ]iThis ordering makes every row and column of the later 12 × 12 matrices comparable.

The model-scale baseline has a 0.15 m clear waterline gap and 0.8 m water depth. BEM uses the wetted surfaces. For display, the 3D scene uses the full FCS exterior and adds visual-only USV topsides and a closed deck above the original wetted mesh.

In the mesh figure, cyan marks FCS panels and orange marks USV panels. The hydrodynamic calculation uses these discrete surfaces with free-surface boundary conditions.

02 / HYDROSTATICS

Build M and C Around Static Equilibrium Completed

For each body, define mass, center of gravity, displacement, draft, and moments of inertia. The rigid-body matrix M represents inertial force from acceleration; the hydrostatic matrix C represents the linear restoring force and moment from a displacement about equilibrium. Combine the two existing 6 × 6 sets in the agreed order to obtain 12 × 12 M and C. Fluid coupling enters later through BEM matrices A and B.

M = diag(MUSV, MFCS),   C = diag(CUSV, CFCS)Here diag means block-diagonal assembly. M and C themselves contain no cross-body fluid interaction.

The current FCS mass is 10.464 kg with about 80 mm draft. The equivalent USV mass is 3.000 kg with about 40.09 mm draft. These design inputs and assumptions need comparison with physical mass, center-of-gravity, and free-decay measurements.

Data: 01_Hydrodynamics/02_Initial_Sizing/results/hydrostatic_parameters.csv. The assembled M and C appear on the Hydrodynamic Matrices tab.

03 / MULTIBODY BOUNDARY ELEMENT METHOD

Solve Fluid Interaction in One Two-Body BEM Problem Completed

At each angular frequency ω, put both wetted meshes in the same water domain. Solve 12 radiation degrees of freedom and incident-wave diffraction/excitation. The outputs are added mass A(ω), radiation damping B(ω), and complex excitation Fexc(ω,β) for heading β. This baseline contains 17 frequencies (0.5–10 rad/s) and 12 headings from 0° to 330° in 30° steps.

A = [AUU   AUF;   AFU   AFF],   B = [BUU   BUF;   BFU   BFF]U = USV and F = FCS. The upper-right and lower-left blocks describe how motion of one body changes fluid force on the other.

Waves radiated by USV motion act on FCS, and vice versa. Two unrelated single-body solves cannot produce these cross-body blocks. Select a frequency on the Hydrodynamic Matrices tab to inspect their values.

Data: 01_Hydrodynamics/05_BEM/MultiBody/baseline_full/coupled_hydrodynamics.npz.

04 / CUMMINS EQUATION — THEORY

Why Does a Broadband Time Model Need Radiation Memory? Theory; not accepted yet

For arbitrary time-varying motion, waves radiated in the past act back on both bodies later. The Cummins equation separates instantaneous inertia from the history of velocity. In this two-body system, the memory kernel K(t) is 12 × 12 and must retain cross-body terms.

(M + A∞) q̈(t) + ∫0t K(t − τ) q̇(τ) dτ + C q(t) = Fexc(t) + Fother(t)Shown for zero pre-history. A∞ is infinite-frequency added mass; Fother may later include mooring, control, and contact forces.
K(t) = (2/π) ∫0∞ B(ω) cos(ωt) dωThis is the ideal continuous-frequency relation. Actual BEM provides a finite discrete grid, so extrapolation, fitting, and independent validation are required.

Radiation memory can be approximated with internal state z: ż = Arz + Brq̇, with memory force approximately Crz + Drq̇. The structural and radiation states together then form a broadband state-space model.

05 / FREQUENCY-DOMAIN COUPLED DYNAMICS

Main Result: Solve the Full 12DOF Frequency Equation Completed

At each computed frequency ω and heading β, combine BEM coefficients with rigid-body and hydrostatic terms in one dynamic-impedance matrix. Let the incident wave be η(t) = Re{a eiωt}. Q is the complex motion RAO per unit wave amplitude; its phase sets the timing of each body relative to the wave.

Z(ω) = −ω² [M + A(ω)] + iω B(ω) + C
Z(ω) Q(ω,β) = Fexc(ω,β)Fexc is excitation per unit incident-wave amplitude, and q(t) = Re{a Q eiωt}. The code uses a numerical pseudoinverse at ill-conditioned frequencies.

A and B retain their cross-body blocks, so Q contains all 12 body motions. A separate block-diagonal, uncoupled baseline helps quantify how fluid interaction changes the RAO.

Code: 02_Dynamics_6DOF/internal/solve_frequency.py. Explore the result on the Motion and Relative Response and Hydrodynamic Matrices tabs.

06 / FREQUENCY-LOCAL TIME DOMAIN

Write the Selected Regular Wave as a 24-State System Completed

To check integration of both 6DOF bodies, choose a BEM frequency ω₀ and hold A₀ = A(ω₀) and B₀ = B(ω₀) fixed. With velocity v = q̇ and state x = [q; v], integrate a first-order ordinary differential equation.

H = M + A₀,   x = [q; v] ∈ ℝ24
ẋ = [ 0   I ; −H−1C   −H−1B₀ ] x + [ 0 ; H−1 ] fwave(t)
fwave(t) = a Re{Fexc(ω₀,β)eiω₀t}Semicolons in the matrix notation separate block rows. This fixed-frequency state space has no Cummins radiation-memory states.

The integrator starts from the steady-state displacement and velocity given by the RAO. Its trajectory over multiple periods is compared at each time with Re{aQeiω₀t}. This checks state ordering, excitation phase, and the time-domain implementation. The web animation uses the same Q for its regular-wave replay.

07 / INTERFACE KINEMATICS

Convert Body Motion to Relative Docking Motion Completed

Docking depends on the distance and orientation between the FCS arm base and the USV receiver. Neither point is at its body's center of gravity, so roll, pitch, and yaw also move the interfaces. Offsets from each center of gravity form a 6 × 12 small-angle transform T.

δp = δxUSV + θUSV × rrecv − δxFCS − θFCS × rarm
δθ = θUSV − θFCS,   Qrel = TQrrecv and rarm are the static receiver and arm-base offsets from their respective centers of gravity.

The static relative position is stored separately. Qrel is an increment about equilibrium, not absolute separation. The Motion and Relative Response tab shows the x/y/z relative RAOs; the 3D scene uses full rotated point positions for its live geometric distance and angle.

Code: interface_transform() in 02_Dynamics_6DOF/internal/solve_frequency.py.

08 / VERIFICATION AND SCOPE

What Has Been Checked, and What Comes Next? Partial verification

Completed numerical checks

  1. Block-diagonal assembly of the separate single-body results reproduces their original RAOs.
  2. Cross-body A and B blocks are nonzero; reciprocity residuals are about 0.36% and 0.79%.
  3. Coupled RAOs differ from the uncoupled baseline, showing the effect of fluid interaction.
  4. Selected 24-state regular-wave integrations agree with same-frequency complex RAOs.

Model and experiment still needed

  1. Measure physical mass, inertia, center of gravity, and free-decay motion to identify viscous damping.
  2. Improve and revalidate the 12 × 12 broadband radiation-memory fit before accepting a Cummins time model.
  3. Add mooring, propulsion/station keeping, arm, magnetic capture, and contact models.
  4. Compare RAOs and docking-interface motion with tank or field measurements.
The current HTML animation shows model-scale, single-frequency regular-wave steady-state motion from linear potential flow. It displays body meshes and an illustrative incident wave, not the diffracted wave field around the hulls. It does not establish a safe operating envelope.

Suggested reading order: follow this workflow, inspect cross-body A/B blocks on Hydrodynamic Matrices, examine RAOs on Motion and Relative Response, and then view the selected wave in 3D Simulation. External theory reference: WEC-Sim Theory Manual.
